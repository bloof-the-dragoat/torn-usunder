# Torn Usunder

> *A world of beastfolk, buried dragons, and seven forces that never learned to be gentle.*

**Torn Usunder** is a work-in-progress fantasy storyboard and worldbuilding vault. It is set in a beastfolk world around **2060–2100**, where magic is a language, leylines power the cities, and seven world-altering **Calamities** each live inside a single host.

> 🚧 **Status:** Storyboarding phase. Lore, characters, factions, and the power system are being built before the first chapters are drafted. Everything here is subject to change.

---

## The Premise

Bloovrn Graymark (nicknamed Bloof) is a dragon-goat hybrid who escaped a laboratory as a child. They were turned into the host of **Wrath**, one of the seven Calamities. Now they are trying to live a normal life, support the adoptive mother who raised them, and keep their past hidden in a world that already distrusts dragon blood.

Then the ancient leylines begin to stir. Gods, Apostles, corporations, and factions start searching for Calamity hosts. Bloof has to decide whether Wrath is a curse that made them a monster, or a power they can choose to use on their own terms. Does it matter?

---

## The World at a Glance

- **Beastfolk society.** Many species, lineages, and hybrids share one world. Biology, ancestry, and prejudice shape who gets opportunities.
- **Beast gods.** Divine powers tied to broad domains, worshipped through temples and served by Apostles.
- **Leylines.** Natural magical currents that power infrastructure and magi-tech. Overuse has ecological, magical, and political consequences.
- **Magi-tech.** Rune devices, resonance crystals, and corporate-built systems make magic repeatable. They also make it something that can be owned, sold, and controlled.
- **Dragon blood.** Respected, feared, and distrusted, depending on who you ask.

## Magic in Brief

> Magic is a language of effects. Mana is the fuel. Affinity is the native accent. Skill is how well someone speaks it.

- Most beastfolk can learn basic magic. Access to teachers, materials, and safety matters as much as raw talent.
- Affinity affects efficiency, not whether something is possible.
- Specialization creates mastery.
- Every strong ability has a limit, a cost, and a counter.
- Power does not equal automatic victory. Preparation, terrain, teamwork, and technology can beat raw strength.
- Magic is regulated, licensed, and ranked. Classification systems like the **Civic Arcana Reach Index (CARI)** raise questions of who gets to decide how dangerous a person is.

---

## Themes

- Personhood versus usefulness
- Power as a curse, a tool, or a choice
- Prejudice, lineage, and who gets to be "normal"
- Found family and quiet survival
- Who controls magic, and who pays for it
